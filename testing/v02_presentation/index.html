The task: give every charge cluster its flash time

  • ND-LAr pile-up: many interactions per spill share one detector — each charge cluster must be matched to the light flash (t0) it produced.
  • Per cluster, an ML model (perceiver) predicts its light image: the waveform pattern it would produce on every channel of its TPC(s).
  • Placement = slide that template along time so that the sum of all placed templates explains the recorded light.
  • Success metric: a hit is correct if |t0reco − t0true| ≤ 10 ticks; report the energy-weighted fraction.
loss(s) = Σch,t w · ( clip(model + shift(template, s)) − actual )²,   w = 1 / (200 · max(actual, 100))

Poisson weights (200 ADC ≈ 1 photon); prediction clipped at the 60780 ADC saturation point; scanned over all shifts at once with an FFT.

One TPC: recorded light (blue), model light from all placed clusters (orange), and the charge tracks in between.

Why order matters: place what you are sure about first

The v0.1 weakness

  • Clusters were placed one-by-one in an arbitrary (label) order.
  • An early cluster is scanned against a nearly empty model — every real flash is unexplained, so several candidate positions look equally good.
  • One early mistake poisons the model for everything placed after it.

The v0.2 intuition

  • Most constrained first: showers (as before), then tracks crossing ≥3 TPCs — their multi-TPC pattern pins them almost uniquely.
  • What remains: 1–2-TPC clusters ≥50 MeV — the genuinely ambiguous pool.
  • Within the pool, clusters of comparable energy compete for the same flashes — so don't guess an order: try the orders.

Energy-banded permutation batches

  • Seed = most energetic unplaced cluster (energy E₁).
  • Batch = pool mates sharing a TPC with the seed and E > (1−s)·E₁ — the ones it can be confused with. Cap: 5.
  • Try all orderings (≤ 5! = 120); each ordering places greedily at its scan optimum given everything placed so far.
  • Keep the ordering with the lowest total loss; commit all but the last-placed; the last returns to the pool and is re-examined with the next energy band.

Band width s swept 0.2 → 0.9: accuracy saturates at s = 0.6 — and the N=5 cap keeps even s = 0.9 at ~5 s/event.

Two physics ingredients inside every scan

1 · Cosine matched filter — "does the leftover light look like me?"

  • At any candidate shift, compare the cluster's channel pattern at its pulse peak with the pattern of unexplained light there.
  • χ² alone is dominated by the model's ~20–30% amplitude systematics on bright walls; the shape across channels is far more reliable.
  • Used softly: score = loss × (1 + (1 − cos)). A hard cut hurts — never forbid, only penalise.

2 · Poisson overflow — "you cannot claim more light than there is"

  • Predicted > actual is physically impossible (light adds up); under-prediction is always possible (someone else's light).
  • zpois = (pred − actual) / √(200 · max(actual, 100)) at the pulse-peak tick, prediction clipped at saturation.
  • Trigger: ≥3 channels with excess ≥ 500 ADC and zpois > 2, OR cos(own, residual) < 0.75 → this placement is suspect.

Trigger operating point: catches ~50% of wrong placements at ~12% false-alarm — and false alarms only cost a re-check.

A wrongly placed track: predicted (purple+gray) overshoots the recorded light (black) on many channels at once — the overflow signature.

Placement police: no overflow allowed, at the moment of placement

  • Downstream stages (large-cluster association, spatial/light rescue) used to place and move clusters unchecked — a wrong move can plant a wall of 10σ overflow and nobody objects.
  • Now: after each downstream stage, every newly placed or moved cluster is probed at its t₀ — in energy order, against the live model.

  • Checking only the ~18 brightest channels works equally well — the bright channels are the ones the ML predicts reliably.

Summary & next step

  • Constrained-first, permutation-based placement + a physics overflow trigger + immediate policing of every downstream placement:

95.1% → 97.25%
(clusters ≥ 50 MeV: 97.4% at full coverage; quiet TPCs: > 99.4%)

  • Every mechanism is loss-audited: nothing is adopted unless the physical light model improves.
  • The trigger + saving machinery generalises: it fixed v0.1 placements, v0.2 placements, and downstream moves alike.

The one remaining frontier is the showering region. Our mistakes are confined to TPCs dominated by large showers / dense activity, where the whole light wall is lit and template matching degenerates. A dedicated shower-recognition path is the next development: detect that a TPC is shower-dominated and hand it to a separate algorithm rather than the standard scan.

The target of the next algorithm: a 2.8 GeV TPC where dense activity misleads whole clusters at once (red = wrong).
